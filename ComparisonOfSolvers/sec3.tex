\section{Solver Specifications}

This section provides a detailed description of the mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) solvers evaluated in our comparative study, with emphasis on their underlying optimization techniques. Modern MILP solvers rely on a combination of linear programming (LP) methods, such as the simplex algorithm \cite{dantzig1963linear} and interior-point methods \cite{karmarkar1984interior}, together with tree-based search strategies, most notably branch-and-bound \cite{land1960automatic} and its extensions. These approaches are typically enhanced with cutting-plane techniques \cite{gomory1958outline}, resulting in the widely used branch-and-cut framework \cite{nemhauser1988integer,wolsey1998integer}.

\subsection{GLPK (GNU Linear Programming Kit)}

GLPK is an open-source linear and integer programming solver developed within the GNU project \cite{glpk2024}. In our experiments, version 4.65 is used. For LP problems, GLPK employs the revised simplex method \cite{dantzig1963linear}, while MILP instances are handled via a branch-and-cut framework that combines branch-and-bound \cite{land1960automatic} with cutting planes \cite{gomory1958outline}. Although GLPK provides deterministic and reliable solutions, it lacks many advanced heuristics and parallelization features found in modern commercial solvers. Consequently, its performance is generally limited on large-scale MILP instances, but it remains a widely used baseline in academic research due to its accessibility and transparency.

\subsection{HiGHS (High-performance Interior-point Solver)}

HiGHS is a modern open-source optimization solver developed at the University of Edinburgh \cite{highs2023}. Version 0.9 is used in this study. It incorporates multiple LP solution techniques, including dual and primal simplex methods \cite{dantzig1963linear} as well as interior-point methods \cite{karmarkar1984interior}. For MILP problems, HiGHS implements a parallel branch-and-bound framework \cite{land1960automatic}, enhanced with presolve reductions and heuristic search strategies. Its design emphasizes computational efficiency and scalability, making it competitive with more established solvers, particularly in multi-core environments.

\subsection{SCIP (Solving Constraint Integer Programs)}

SCIP is an advanced open-source framework for constraint integer programming developed by the Zuse Institute Berlin \cite{scip2023}. Version 7.0 is used in our experiments. SCIP extends the classical MILP paradigm by integrating constraint programming techniques with branch-and-cut methods \cite{nemhauser1988integer}. It employs a branch-and-bound search tree \cite{land1960automatic}, strengthened by cutting planes \cite{gomory1958outline}, primal heuristics, and symmetry-breaking mechanisms. Additionally, SCIP supports nonlinear and quadratic constraints, making it suitable for a broader class of optimization problems. Among open-source solvers, SCIP is often regarded as one of the most powerful due to its flexibility and advanced algorithmic components.

\subsection{Gurobi Optimizer}

Gurobi is a state-of-the-art commercial optimization solver developed by Gurobi Optimization \cite{gurobi2024}. Version 10.0 is used in this work. It utilizes a highly optimized branch-and-cut framework \cite{nemhauser1988integer,wolsey1998integer}, combined with advanced presolve techniques, cutting-plane separation, and primal-dual heuristics. For LP relaxations, Gurobi supports both simplex methods \cite{dantzig1963linear} and barrier (interior-point) methods \cite{karmarkar1984interior}. The solver is designed to exploit parallel architectures efficiently and provides extensive parameter tuning capabilities. Due to its performance and robustness, Gurobi is widely used as a benchmark in both academic and industrial applications.

\subsection{CPLEX (IBM CPLEX Optimization Studio)}

CPLEX is a commercial optimization solver developed by IBM with a long history of algorithmic development \cite{cplex2024}. Version 12.10 is considered in this study. It supports multiple LP solution strategies, including primal and dual simplex methods \cite{dantzig1963linear} and barrier methods with crossover \cite{karmarkar1984interior}. For MILP problems, CPLEX employs a branch-and-cut approach \cite{nemhauser1988integer}, integrating cutting planes \cite{gomory1958outline}, preprocessing, and probing techniques. It also incorporates advanced heuristics and parallel processing capabilities. CPLEX remains one of the most widely used solvers in enterprise-level optimization and large-scale industrial applications.

\subsection{Experimental Configuration}
All solvers were executed with default parameters to ensure fair and reproducible comparison. This approach reflects typical usage patterns where practitioners rely on default settings without extensive parameter tuning. Each solver was allowed unlimited computation time (no time limits were imposed) to find optimal solutions. Experiments were conducted on an 11th Gen Intel Core i7-1165G7 processor operating at 2.80 GHz with 32 GB of system RAM, running Ubuntu Linux. This ensures all solvers had equal access to computational resources, with no artificial constraints or advantages.
